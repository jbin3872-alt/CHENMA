Moving heavy appliances and equipment up stairs is one of the most physically demanding jobs in logistics and moving. The choice between an electric stair climber and a manual hand truck is really a choice between investing in equipment and spending on labor - plus the hidden cost of injuries.

Direct Answer: The Selection Rule

If your team carries loads above 150-200kg up stairs more than a few times a week, an electric stair climber such as the LP800-1 (400kg capacity) pays for itself quickly through reduced labor, faster cycles, and far lower injury risk.

The Cost Recovery Logic

Payback Period (months) = (Electric Climber Price - Hand Truck Price) / Monthly Labor Savings

  • Labor savings per job: An electric climber typically reduces a two-person stair move to one person, saving 1-2 hours of labor per heavy delivery.
  • Volume effect: At 3+ heavy stair deliveries per week, monthly savings in labor hours alone can cover the equipment cost difference within months.
  • Injury cost avoidance: Manual handling injuries (back strain, dropped loads) carry medical, insurance, and downtime costs that are often the largest hidden expense.
  • Asset longevity: The LP800-1's manganese steel frame and 3-5 year lithium battery life make it a long-term investment, not a consumable.

When a Manual Hand Truck Still Makes Sense

  • Rare use: Fewer than one stair move per week, where the equipment would sit idle.
  • Light loads: Items well under 100kg that a single strong operator handles comfortably.
  • Tight budget: When capital expenditure is not available, a hand truck is the lowest entry cost.
