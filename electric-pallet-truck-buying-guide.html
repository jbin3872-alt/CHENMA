Electric Pallet Truck Buying Guide

Choose the right floor-level pallet mover by starting with the load, fork fit, turning aisle and battery duty cycle. This guide also explains when a pallet truck is the right tool — and when a mast-equipped stacker is the safer choice.

September 14, 20267 min read

Direct Answer

The 4 dimensions that decide an electric pallet truck

  1. Rated capacity. Select a rated load above the heaviest pallet, including uneven or shifting loads.
  2. Fork specifications. Check fork length, outside width, fork spacing and entry height against your pallet openings.
  3. Turning space. Measure aisle width, rack-end clearance, doorway width and the truck's turning radius before ordering.
  4. Battery runtime. Match battery voltage and amp-hours to shift length, route distance, load weight and charging access.

If the job includes rack storage or repeated vertical placement, compare a stacker as well. A floor-level pallet truck and a mast-equipped stacker are different tools.

Forks, Turning Space and Battery: The Practical Fit Check

Forks

Measure pallet entry openings and load length. Forks should support the load evenly without excessive overhang. Ask for fork length, outside width, spacing and minimum entry height in writing.

Turning

Mark the narrowest aisle and make a loaded mock turn. Include the pallet's rear overhang, doorway clearance, rack-end space and pedestrian exclusion zone.

Runtime

Compare voltage and Ah with your duty cycle. Lithium batteries can support opportunity charging; lead-acid systems need a suitable charging area and planned charge time. Use the supplier's loaded-route estimate.

Safe Operating Checklist

1

Inspect before use — forks, wheels, controls, emergency stop, battery and warning labels.

2

Confirm the load — weight, center of gravity, pallet condition and stable stacking.

3

Set the route — clear pedestrians, check ramps and keep visibility unobstructed.

4

Travel low and slow — keep forks/load low and reduce speed before turns.

5

Use the horn — signal at doorways, blind corners and intersections.

6

Park safely — lower forks fully, apply the brake and isolate power when unattended.

8 Questions to Ask a Supplier

1. Capacity: What is the rated load and capacity margin at the load center I use?
2. Forks: What are fork length, width, spacing and entry height?
3. Turning: What is the turning radius or minimum aisle for my loaded pallet?
4. Battery: What voltage, Ah rating, runtime and charge time apply to my duty cycle?
5. Safety: Which emergency stop, speed control, brake and warning systems are included?
6. Floor: What gradients, floor joints and ramp conditions are acceptable?
7. Documentation: Are manuals, certifications, spare-parts lists and charger details supplied?
8. Support: What warranty, service response and replacement battery support are available?

Frequently Asked Questions

What four specifications should I compare when choosing an electric pallet truck?

Compare rated capacity, fork length and width, turning space, and battery runtime first. Then confirm lift height, charger compatibility, safety controls and service support for the intended warehouse route.

What is the difference between an electric pallet truck and a stacker?

An electric pallet truck is primarily for moving pallets horizontally at floor level. A stacker adds a mast for vertical lifting and storage. Select a pallet truck for short horizontal moves and a stacker when you must place loads on racks or at elevated positions.

Which Chenma stacker specifications are verified for pallet workflows?

The Chenma CMAE-F04 Stand-on Stacker is rated at 500 kg, lifts 2 m, uses a 48V / 32A lead-acid battery and has a 1500 W motor. The Chenma CMSE-S01 Semi-Electric Stacker is rated at 1000 kg, lifts 1.6 m, uses a 48V / 15AH lithium battery and has an 800 W motor.

How can I estimate electric pallet truck battery runtime?

Start with the battery voltage and amp-hour rating, then compare the result with your shift length, load weight, travel distance, floor condition and charging opportunities. Ask the supplier for a duty-cycle estimate based on your actual route rather than relying on an unloaded runtime claim.
