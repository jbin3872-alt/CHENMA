How to Choose a Hydraulic Lifting Table

A hydraulic lifting table is one of the simplest ways to remove strain from loading and assembly lines — but only if you size it correctly. This guide walks through capacity, platform and travel, mobility, and power so the table you buy matches the job you do every day.

September 7, 2026 7 min read

Direct Answer

4 Decision Dimensions for Choosing a Hydraulic Lifting Table

  1. Load capacity. Size the rated load above your heaviest real load — never run a table at 100% of its limit.
  2. Platform size and travel. The tabletop must fit your goods and pallets, and the lift stroke must reach your target working height.
  3. Fixed vs mobile. Permanent workstation or moving loads between stations changes the whole machine design.
  4. Power source. Electric tables need a battery or mains supply; hydraulic pump tables suit lighter, occasional duties.

Start with the heaviest load and the highest working height, then filter by power source and mobility — every other specification follows from those four answers.

Lifting Table vs Forklift / Stacker: Division of Work

They both lift — but they solve different problems. Choose the tool that fits the workflow, not the biggest machine.

Criterion Hydraulic lifting table Forklift / stacker

Operator skillLow — press a button or pump a leverHigher — trained and certified operators
SpaceStays in one footprint at the workstationNeeds aisles and turning space
Rule of thumbLoads come to the liftThe lift goes to the loads

Fixed vs Mobile: Decision Framework

Three questions decide it: does the load position change? Is the floor smooth? How often is the table used?

Choose a fixed table when

  • The workstation never moves and loads arrive at the same position.
  • You need the lowest platform height for stable, high-capacity lifting.
  • Mains power is available and a permanent electrical connection is acceptable.
  • Maximizing rigidity and load capacity matters more than portability.

Choose a mobile table when

  • Loads move between stations, or a table must serve several lines.
  • You work in different zones across a shift and need one unit.
  • Battery-powered operation (e.g. 12V lead-acid) is preferred to avoid wiring.
  • The floor is smooth enough for casters to roll loaded tables safely.

8 Questions to Ask Before You Buy

Work through this checklist with any supplier — including us — before ordering.

1

Capacity margin — is the rated load at least 120% of your heaviest real load?

2

Travel & working height — does the lift stroke reach your target height from the lowest platform position?

3

Platform size — do your pallets, totes or assemblies sit fully on the tabletop?

4

Power source — battery (voltage and Ah) or mains? Is the motor power (W) adequate for duty frequency?

5

Safety devices — overload protection, anti-pinch edges, limit switches and emergency stop fitted?

6

Mobility — fixed or mobile? Caster load rating and floor conditions confirmed?

7

Certification — CE certification and documentation for your market available?

8

After-sales — spares, manuals and warranty response time defined in writing?

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a hydraulic lifting table and when should I use one?

A hydraulic lifting table is a ground-level lifting device that raises loads to a working height for loading, assembly or material handling. Unlike an aerial work platform, a lifting table carries goods, not people. Use one when you repeatedly lift heavy items a short distance at a fixed workstation.

What are the specifications of the Chenma CMLM-E12 electric table lift?

The Chenma CMLM-E12 Electric Table Lift offers load capacities of 200 kg, 300 kg or 1000 kg with lift heights of 126 cm, 90 cm or 100 cm respectively, powered by 500 W, 800 W or 1000 W motors and a 12V lead-acid battery.

What is the load capacity of the Chenma CMLM-H10 hydraulic table lift?

The Chenma CMLM-H10 Hydraulic Table Lift carries 500 kg and provides a lift height of 30 to 40 cm. The CMLM-H11 offers 150 kg (21-22 cm lift) or 300 kg (28-30 cm lift) configurations.

Should I choose a fixed or mobile lifting table?

Choose a fixed table for a permanent workstation with repeated lifting at one position. Choose a mobile table when loads must be moved between stations or loaded directly from a cart or pallet truck, and confirm the floor is smooth enough for the casters.

Disclaimer: This guide provides general industry guidance for selecting hydraulic lifting tables. Lifting tables are designed to carry goods — never use one to raise people unless it is explicitly certified for passenger use. Always confirm load ratings, travel, power source and safety devices with the manufacturer before purchase, and follow the operator's manual after installation.
