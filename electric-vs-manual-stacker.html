Every warehouse manager faces the same question: should I invest in an electric stacker or keep using manual ones? The answer depends on your lifting frequency, labor costs, and long-term throughput goals.

Direct Answer: The Selection Rule

If your team performs more than 40-50 lifts per shift, or operates in an environment with high labor costs, an electric stacker such as the CMSE-S03 typically pays for itself through reduced operator fatigue and faster cycles.

ROI Calculation Logic

Use this simple framework to estimate your payback period:

Payback Period (months) = (Electric Price − Manual Price) ÷ Monthly Labor Savings

  • Monthly Labor Savings = Time saved per lift (minutes) × Lifts per month ÷ 60 × Hourly labor cost
  • Time saved per lift: Remote-controlled electric lifting eliminates manual pumping and walking, typically saving 1-3 minutes per lift cycle in practice.
  • Secondary savings: reduced injury risk, fewer damaged pallets, and consistent lifting quality.

For a facility running 100 lifts per day with a labor cost of $15/hour, a 2-minute saving per lift translates to roughly 100 hours of labor saved per month - which can offset the price difference between an electric and manual stacker within months rather than years.

When the Manual Stacker Still Makes Sense

  • Very low lift frequency: Under 10-15 lifts per day, manual operation remains practical.
  • Budget constraints: When upfront capital is the primary decision factor.
  • Light loads: For loads well below capacity with short travel distances.
