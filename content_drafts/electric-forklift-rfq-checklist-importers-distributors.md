# Electric Forklift RFQ Checklist for Importers and Distributors: Specifications, Documents and Supplier Questions

**Direct answer:** A useful electric forklift RFQ should connect the buyer's real load and site to one exact product configuration, then ask the supplier to confirm every technical, document and delivery field in writing. Start with rated capacity and load center, required lift height and residual-capacity information, operating form, forks, dimensions, battery and charger, electrical conditions, work cycle, spare parts, after-sales documents, destination-market files, packaging and transport data, and any OEM/ODM or label-language requirements. Do not ask only for a model name and a price; ask for an auditable quotation package.

This **electric forklift RFQ checklist** is written for importers, distributors and project procurement teams. It is not another general capacity or battery selection guide. Use the existing [electric forklift category](https://www.chenmalifting.com/electric-forklifts.html), [semi-electric forklift category](https://www.chenmalifting.com/semi-electric-forklifts.html) and [electric forklift selection guide](https://www.chenmalifting.com/blog/electric-forklift-selection-guide.html) to build an initial shortlist. Then use this document to make the supplier response complete and comparable.

## 1. Define the RFQ object before asking for a quotation

An RFQ should identify what is being quoted, not merely what industry the buyer serves. State whether the request is for a full-electric forklift, a semi-electric stacker, a replacement unit, a distributor sample, a project batch or an OEM/ODM program. Give the destination country and intended application, but do not assume that a category name defines the final drive, lift or control configuration.

Use a model-specific starting point when useful. The [CMAE-F06 precision forklift page](https://www.chenmalifting.com/products/cmae-f06-precision-forklift-500kg-21m.html) records 500 kg, a maximum lift height of 210 cm, a 48V / 32A lead-acid battery and a 95 cm turning radius. The [CMAE-F07 heavy-duty forklift page](https://www.chenmalifting.com/products/cmae-f07-heavy-duty-2000kg-3m.html) records 2000 kg, a maximum lift height of 300 cm, a 48V / 32A lead-acid battery, an unloaded speed of 12 km/h, a full-load lifting speed of 15 cm/s and a 95 cm turning radius. These are product-record facts, not interchangeable specifications for every forklift.

For a semi-electric project, the [CMSE-S08 universal stacker page](https://www.chenmalifting.com/products/cmse-s08-universal-stacker-1-ton-up-to-35m.html) lists 1000 / 1500 / 2000 kg and 1600 / 2000 / 2500 / 3000 / 3500 mm options, with DC 12V and a 2.2 kW DC motor. The RFQ must identify the selected load-height combination. The [CMSE-S01 page](https://www.chenmalifting.com/products/cmse-s01-semi-electric-stacker-1-ton-16m.html) can be used when a 1000 kg, 1.6 m semi-electric reference is relevant. Confirm all configuration-dependent fields in writing.

## 2. Electric forklift specifications to include

The technical section should make the supplier answer the same fields for every shortlisted model. Use the following checklist.

### Rated capacity and load center

State the normal load, maximum complete load, package dimensions and load center information available to the buyer. Ask the supplier to state rated capacity for the quoted configuration and explain which load-center assumption applies. Do not substitute a generic capacity for the actual package or attachment. If the load center is unknown, mark it as an open item and request written engineering confirmation.

### Lift height and residual capacity

State the required fork height, storage or placement height and any clearance constraint. Ask for the maximum lift field for the exact configuration and request the applicable residual-capacity information at the intended lift height and load center. A maximum lift number alone does not establish capacity at every height. If no residual-capacity curve or table is provided, write “confirm in writing” rather than inventing one.

### Operating form and controls

Specify whether the task requires seated, stand-on, walk-behind, manual, button, wired-remote or wireless-remote operation, where the product record documents those fields. Ask the supplier to describe travel, lifting, steering and control functions for the quoted model. Do not infer the operation from “full-electric” or “semi-electric” alone.

### Forks, attachments and dimensions

List fork length, fork width, adjustable range, fork material, carriage or attachment needs and the pallet/support format. Include overall length, width, height, turning information, mast position and dimensions with or without forks where relevant. A product page may list one dimension convention; ask the supplier to confirm the measurement basis for the quotation.

### Site dimensions and work cycle

Attach doorway, aisle, turning, rack, loading and charging-area measurements. State floor conditions, route changes and pedestrian interfaces. Describe the expected work cycle: loads per shift, travel distance, lift frequency, operating hours, pauses and charging windows. Do not turn a work-time field into a guaranteed runtime or duty-cycle promise; ask for configuration-specific confirmation.

### Battery, charger and electrical conditions

State the available electrical supply, plug or connection expectations, battery voltage, capacity and chemistry preference, charger location and charging workflow. Ask whether the quoted package includes the charger, cables, controls and any required protection. Existing records demonstrate that battery fields vary: F06 and F07 list 48V / 32A lead-acid, while other records list lithium, 12V or different lead-acid fields. Do not assume a shared battery specification across a product family.

## 3. Documents and supplier questions for importers

A complete RFQ should ask for documents that support technical review, customs preparation, installation and after-sales planning. Document names and availability depend on the destination market and the final configuration, so ask the supplier to identify what is available and what requires local review.

Request the following in writing:

- A model-specific quotation with configuration, quantity, validity period and included scope.
- A technical datasheet matching the quoted model and options.
- Dimension drawings, load-center information and residual-capacity documentation where applicable.
- Battery, charger and electrical information, including connection requirements.
- User, operation, inspection and maintenance instructions in the agreed language.
- Recommended spare-parts list, wear-parts list and part-identification method.
- Warranty scope, warranty duration, exclusions, claim process and response route.
- After-sales contact, troubleshooting process, service training and remote-support options.
- Packing list, gross/net weight, package dimensions and shipping marks.
- Commercial invoice and other export or transport documents required for the order.
- Destination-market documentation requested by the importer, customs broker or local authority.
- Any declaration, test report or certification document the supplier can provide for the exact configuration; do not assume a certification scope that is not stated.

Ask the supplier to separate “included,” “optional,” “not available” and “confirm in writing.” This makes a quotation easier to audit than a single unqualified promise.

## 4. Packaging, transport and destination-market checks

Importers should collect transport information before approving the commercial package. Ask for package dimensions, gross weight, number of packages, loading orientation, lifting points, moisture protection, shock or tilt markings, battery shipping treatment and any special handling instruction. Confirm whether the stated dimensions refer to the machine, the packed machine or a particular option.

Provide the destination country, port or delivery point, consignee information and any broker requirements that the supplier must support. Ask which documents will accompany the shipment and which must be arranged by the importer or a local professional. Do not promise a delivery date, duty result or certification outcome without an actual supplier and destination review.

For site preparation, link the RFQ to the [electric forklift workplace layout safety guide](https://www.chenmalifting.com/blog/electric-forklift-workplace-layout-safety.html) and the [electric forklift safety guide](https://www.chenmalifting.com/electric-forklift-safety-guide.html). Those resources address route measurements and operating controls; this RFQ focuses on turning the required information into supplier-confirmed fields.

## 5. OEM, ODM, spare parts and after-sales questions

Distributors and project buyers should separate a standard purchase from an OEM/ODM request. State whether the requirement is a logo, color, housing change, control-label change, packaging change, manual translation, private label, product naming change or a deeper engineering modification. Ask which changes affect testing, documents, spare parts, warranty and lead-time confirmation. Do not assume that a logo or label change has no technical or document impact.

For after-sales planning, request a recommended initial spare-parts package, part numbers, service intervals as documented, troubleshooting guidance, warranty workflow and contact escalation. Ask how replacement parts are identified when a batch contains different configurations. If a field is not in the supplier's standard record, require “confirm in writing” before treating it as part of the commercial offer.

Also ask the supplier to identify the document revision, configuration code and responsible contact on each after-sales file. This prevents a distributor from matching a replacement part or warranty instruction to the wrong variant. Record whether training material, inspection forms and service diagrams are included, optional or unavailable.

## 6. Open items requiring written confirmation

Create a separate open-items block in every RFQ. It should include at least:

1. Rated capacity at the required load center and lift height.
2. Residual-capacity curve, table or written engineering confirmation.
3. Exact fork dimensions, attachment compatibility and measurement basis.
4. Overall machine dimensions for the quoted configuration.
5. Turning information and route suitability for the measured site.
6. Battery chemistry, voltage, capacity, charger and electrical connection.
7. Work-cycle suitability, runtime or charging expectations where not documented.
8. Warranty scope, exclusions, spare-parts availability and after-sales response.
9. Destination-market documents and any configuration-specific declaration or report.
10. Packaging dimensions, gross weight, shipping marks and battery transport handling.
11. OEM/ODM change scope, label language, manual language and packaging language.
12. Any price, delivery, certification or performance field not supported by the quotation or source record.

Use the phrase “confirm in writing” for unresolved fields. This is more useful than filling a gap with a standard number from another model.

## 7. Supplier response comparison and CTA

When responses arrive, compare them line by line. Keep the buyer's requested field, supplier response, evidence document, status and follow-up question in one table. Mark each row as confirmed, optional, excluded or open. Do not compare one supplier's complete configuration with another supplier's category-level description.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the most important field in an electric forklift RFQ?

Start with the complete load, rated capacity and load center, then connect them to the required lift height. These fields determine what the supplier must confirm for the quoted configuration; a model name alone is not enough.

### Why should an importer request residual-capacity information?

Maximum lift height and rated capacity do not automatically describe capacity at every lift height or load center. Request the applicable residual-capacity curve, table or written engineering confirmation for the intended configuration.

### What documents should a forklift supplier provide?

Request a model-specific quotation, datasheet, drawings, capacity information, battery and charger details, manuals, spare-parts list, warranty terms, after-sales process, packing data and destination-market documents that apply to the exact configuration. Mark anything unavailable as “confirm in writing.”

### How should distributors ask about OEM or ODM changes?

State the requested logo, color, engineering, packaging, label-language, manual-language or private-label change. Ask the supplier to confirm scope, effect on documents, spare parts, warranty and delivery information in writing.

### What transport information belongs in the RFQ?

Ask for package dimensions, gross and net weight, number of packages, loading orientation, lifting points, shipping marks, moisture protection and battery transport treatment. Confirm which transport and destination documents are supplied and which are the importer's responsibility.

### What should I do when a specification is missing?

List it as an open item and request written confirmation from the supplier or responsible engineering professional. Do not infer price, delivery time, certification scope, residual capacity, battery life or performance from another model.
