# Electric Forklift Workplace Layout Safety: Aisle, Turning and Charging-Zone Planning

## Direct answer: measure the workplace before assigning an electric forklift route

Electric forklift workplace safety starts with a measured site, not a generic aisle number. Record the load, storage height, doorway width, aisle geometry, turning area, floor conditions, charging location and pedestrian interfaces. Then compare those measurements with the exact forklift record and ask the manufacturer to confirm any missing limit. For example, the verified CMAE-F06 and CMAE-F07 records each state a 95 cm turning radius, but their overall dimensions and rated loads are very different. A safe warehouse forklift layout must therefore be checked against the selected model, the load and the local operating rules.

Do not copy a universal minimum aisle width, turning clearance or charging distance from an unrelated machine. If a safety threshold is not stated in the product record, confirm it using local regulations and manufacturer data. The purpose of layout planning is to create a documented match between the workplace and the quoted configuration before equipment is ordered.

## Measure the workplace before choosing a route

Forklift site measurements should describe the places where the machine will travel, stop, turn, lift and charge. Walk the proposed route with a tape measure or approved site-measurement method, and record the result in a drawing or checklist. Measure the narrowest doorway, the usable route width, the corners, the rack or storage face, the loading position, the charging location and any change in floor level. Mark fixed columns, walls, doors, ramps, drains, curbs and areas where people may enter the route.

Measure the load as well as the building. Record the heaviest stated load, load dimensions, pallet or support format, load center information if available, pickup height and placement height. A route that fits an unloaded machine may not fit the machine with a load. The RFQ should carry both the site drawing and the load description so the supplier can review the complete operating condition.

The following site-measurement checklist keeps the review specific:

- Narrowest doorway and gate opening.
- Route width at the narrowest point, measured along the intended travel line.
- Corner geometry and available turning area.
- Rack, shelf or placement height required by the task.
- Floor surface, level changes, slopes and transitions.
- Load length, width, height and stated mass.
- Battery charging location, electrical supply and ventilation conditions.
- Pedestrian crossings, workstations and areas that require a traffic-control decision.
- Emergency access, fire equipment and other fixed features that local rules require the team to protect.

The list does not create a universal clearance value. It creates evidence for the actual machine, load and site.

## Plan aisles, turns and traffic around the selected machine

Electric forklift aisle planning should begin with the machine dimensions and turning information in the exact product record. CMAE-F06 is documented at 500 kg, a maximum lift height of 210 cm and a 95 cm turning radius. CMAE-F07 is documented at 2000 kg, a maximum lift height of 300 cm and the same stated 95 cm turning radius. Their overall dimensions are not the same: F06 is listed at L230 cm / W78 cm / H158 cm, while F07 is listed at L337 cm / W120 cm / H215 cm. The shared turning-radius figure does not make their routes interchangeable.

Draw the route as a sequence of actions rather than as one number. Identify where the operator approaches the load, where the forks enter, where the load is raised, where the machine turns and where the load is placed. Check the longest machine dimension at doors and the widest dimension at narrow passages. At corners, use the documented forklift turning radius as a starting data point and request manufacturer confirmation for the complete turning movement with the selected load and fork setup.

A warehouse forklift layout should show how people and equipment interact. Mark pedestrian crossings, packing stations, loading points and places where the forklift reverses or changes direction. Apply the site traffic plan required by local rules and the employer's procedure. Do not invent a separation distance or claim that an aisle width is safe without applicable rule and manufacturer confirmation.

These pages can help a team organize a shortlist, but the final route decision still belongs to the exact model and site measurements.

## Design the charging zone as part of the layout

Electric forklift charging area safety is a layout question as well as an equipment question. Put the charging location on the same site drawing as the travel route. Record the available electrical supply, charger position, cable path, floor condition, ventilation condition and the way the machine will enter and leave the area. Protect the charging point from conflicts with normal traffic, storage and pedestrian movement according to local requirements.

Use the battery and power fields actually documented for the selected machine. CMAE-F06 and CMAE-F07 are both recorded with 48V / 32A lead-acid batteries. The F06 record lists a 2000 W drive motor and a 2500 W hoist motor. The F07 record lists a 5000 W drive motor and a 4000 W hoist motor. These facts identify the records; they do not establish charging duration, battery life, duty cycle or a universal electrical installation rule.

Before approval, ask the supplier and responsible site professional to confirm the charger, connection method, ventilation, maintenance provisions, signage and emergency arrangements for the selected battery configuration. Follow local electrical, fire and workplace requirements. If the record lacks a charging-zone threshold, write “confirm by local regulations and manufacturer data” rather than guessing a distance.

A practical charging-zone check includes:

- Is the charging position shown on the current site drawing?
- Is the machine route into and out of the zone clear for the selected dimensions?
- Is the available electrical supply recorded for quotation review?
- Are cables, charger controls and pedestrian movement addressed by the site procedure?
- Are ventilation, fire protection, housekeeping and emergency instructions confirmed by the responsible professional?
- Does the RFQ identify the exact battery and charger configuration rather than only the product family?

For operating fundamentals, link this planning work to the [electric forklift safety guide](../electric-forklift-safety-guide.html), while keeping layout-specific decisions tied to the actual site.

## Match the warehouse layout to verified model dimensions

Model selection becomes clearer when the layout review uses documented dimensions instead of category assumptions. CMAE-F06 is a compact full-electric example in the verified records: 500 kg capacity, 210 cm maximum lift height, 48V / 32A lead-acid battery and 95 cm turning radius. CMAE-F07 is a heavier example: 2000 kg capacity, 300 cm maximum lift height, 48V / 32A lead-acid battery, an unloaded speed of 12 km/h, a full-load lifting speed of 15 cm/s and a 95 cm turning radius. The speed fields are product facts, not permission to choose a site operating speed without the applicable workplace rules.

CMSE-S05 is documented at 500 kg, 1.7 m and an outer width of 50 cm. That outer-width field may be relevant when comparing a compact semi-electric option with a measured passage, but it does not replace a complete route review. CMSE-S08 is configuration-sensitive: the selection guide lists 1000 / 1500 / 2000 kg options and 1600 / 2000 / 2500 / 3000 / 3500 mm lifting-height options, with DC 12V and a 2.2 kW DC motor. The specific load-height combination must be confirmed in the RFQ.

## Run a pre-start layout inspection

A layout drawing is useful only when the installed workplace still matches it. Before regular operation, walk the route with the responsible supervisor and compare the drawing with the current floor, racks, doors, charging zone and pedestrian interfaces. Repeat the review after a rack change, new workstation, altered doorway, changed load, new forklift configuration or charging-area modification.

Use this layout inspection checklist:

- The route drawing identifies the exact forklift model and configuration under review.
- The measured doorway, passage, corner and placement points are still accurate.
- The load dimensions and stated mass match the quotation and work task.
- Turning movements have been reviewed against the documented turning radius and manufacturer guidance.
- Pedestrian crossings and interaction points have a site-approved control method.
- The charging zone is shown, accessible and reviewed against local electrical and workplace requirements.
- Missing parameters are written as open confirmation items rather than assumptions.
- Operators receive the site-specific route and charging instructions required by the employer and applicable rules.

This is a planning control, not a replacement for training, risk assessment, inspection or legal compliance. If the site rule and product record use different terms, ask the responsible safety or engineering professional to resolve the requirement before operation.

## Frequently asked questions

The answers below summarize the layout-planning method without creating a generic safety threshold. Apply the exact product record, local rules and manufacturer confirmation to the site under review.

### What should I measure before planning an electric forklift route?

Measure the narrowest doorway and passage, corner geometry, placement height, floor transitions, load dimensions, charging location, electrical supply and pedestrian interfaces. Record the heaviest stated load and the exact machine configuration so the supplier can review the complete route.

### Can I use one aisle plan for CMAE-F06 and CMAE-F07?

Not without a model-specific review. Both records state a 95 cm turning radius, but F06 is listed at L230 cm / W78 cm / H158 cm and 500 kg, while F07 is listed at L337 cm / W120 cm / H215 cm and 2000 kg. Their route, load and turning movements must be checked separately.

### Does a 95 cm turning radius define the complete safe turning space?

No. It is a documented product field for the referenced F06 and F07 records, not a complete universal layout rule. Confirm the full movement, load, forks, floor conditions and applicable site requirements with the manufacturer and responsible safety professional.

### What should an electric forklift charging area review include?

Include the charging position on the site drawing, then confirm the battery and charger configuration, electrical supply, cable path, ventilation, housekeeping, fire protection and emergency arrangements under local requirements. Do not infer charging time, battery life or a universal distance from the battery voltage alone.

### How should I handle CMSE-S08 in a warehouse layout?

Treat S08 as configuration-sensitive. The selection guide lists 1000 / 1500 / 2000 kg and 1600 / 2000 / 2500 / 3000 / 3500 mm options, plus DC 12V and a 2.2 kW DC motor. Ask the RFQ to identify the selected load-height combination before checking the route.

### What if a required site or product parameter is missing?

Write the missing item in the RFQ and request confirmation from the manufacturer or the responsible local professional. Use “confirm by local regulations and manufacturer data” rather than guessing a minimum aisle width, charging distance, residual-capacity curve or other safety threshold.
