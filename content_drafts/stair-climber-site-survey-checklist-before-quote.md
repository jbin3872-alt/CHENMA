# Stair Climber Site Survey Checklist: What to Measure Before Requesting a Quote

A reliable stair climber quotation starts with a reliable site survey. Before asking a supplier to recommend a model, record the staircase geometry, access route, cargo dimensions, load distribution, surface condition and operating environment. A product name or maximum load alone is not enough to confirm whether a route is workable. This stair climber site survey checklist turns a difficult site visit into a structured RFQ that a manufacturer or distributor can review in writing.

The goal is not to approve a machine by guesswork. The goal is to give the supplier enough evidence to identify a suitable configuration, explain open questions and state what must be tested on site. Use the checklist below for warehouses, construction sites, retail deliveries, building maintenance and other commercial material-handling jobs.

## Why a site survey should come before a stair climber quote

A stair climber operates within a complete route, not on a single staircase in isolation. A machine may fit the straight section but fail at a landing, doorway, narrow corridor or transition to the floor. The cargo can also change the practical requirement: a tall appliance, a compact safe and a long machine may have similar weights but very different centres of gravity and turning needs.

A site survey reduces back-and-forth questions and helps a supplier separate confirmed facts from assumptions. It also gives a distributor a repeatable way to qualify customer enquiries before recommending a product. Start with the [electric stair climber category](../stair-climbers.html), then use the site information to decide which product pages require closer review. Do not treat a category description as a site approval or as a substitute for supplier confirmation.

The survey should produce four outputs: a measured route sketch, a cargo profile, a set of clear photographs and a list of questions that require a written answer. If a measurement is unavailable, mark it as unknown rather than filling it with a typical value.

## Measure each stair flight and landing

Use a tape measure or laser measure and record the dimensions for every flight, not only the first visible section. Measure clear stair width between the narrowest fixed obstacles, including handrails, wall projections, pipes and temporary barriers. Record the tread depth and riser height at several points if the stairs are uneven. Note whether nosings are rounded, damaged, slippery or sharply projecting.

Landings deserve the same attention as the steps. Record the landing length and width, the available turning area and the distance from the last riser to the nearest door or wall. For an L-shaped or U-shaped route, sketch the direction of travel and identify whether the cargo must be rotated, repositioned or temporarily parked. Include the width and height of every doorway along the route, including doors that must be opened fully or held clear.

Use this stair climber measurement checklist table during the visit:

| Site item | What to record | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Clear stair width | Narrowest usable width | Confirms machine and cargo clearance |
| Riser height | Typical and maximum observed height | Helps the supplier review stair geometry |
| Tread depth | Typical depth and irregular sections | Affects contact and movement conditions |
| Landing size | Length, width and turning area | Indicates whether repositioning is possible |
| Door openings | Clear width and height | Checks access before and after the stairs |
| Handrails and obstacles | Fixed projections and removable items | Identifies pinch points and clearance limits |

Do not rely on a single photograph to show scale. Add a measurement reference or write the dimension beside the sketch so the supplier can interpret the image correctly.

## Record the complete route, not only the steps

A route survey starts at the loading point and ends where the cargo will be placed. Map every transition: truck or van to pavement, pavement to doorway, doorway to corridor, corridor to elevator or stairs, landing to upper floor and final placement area. Record slopes, thresholds, drains, ramps, loose surfaces and changes in floor height. A route that is suitable inside a building may include a difficult outdoor section before the machine reaches the stairs.

Classify each route section as straight, turning, restricted, uneven or unknown. For a multi-floor job, record how many separate flights are involved and whether the operator must stop on intermediate landings. Note where other people, customers, vehicles or construction activity may cross the route. This information is relevant to scheduling, spotter requirements and safe exclusion zones, but it does not replace the supplier’s operating instructions or local safety procedures.

Photograph the route in travel order. Take wide images for context and close images of thresholds, corners, handrails and damaged surfaces. Name the files consistently, such as `01_loading_area`, `02_lower_door`, `03_first_flight` and `04_upper_landing`. Link each image to the sketch so a supplier can match the photo to the measured location.

For product context, compare the route information with the [commercial and industrial stair climber guide](../blog/electric-stair-climber-commercial-industrial-selection-guide.html), but keep the site survey as the controlling RFQ document. General guidance can explain questions; only a supplier reviewing the actual route can confirm a configuration.

## Measure the cargo, packaging and centre of gravity

The cargo profile should describe the load as it will actually travel. Record gross weight, length, width, height and the dimensions after packaging. If the load is shipped on a pallet, crate or protective frame, measure the complete package rather than the product alone. Note removable parts, protruding handles, wheels, fragile panels and any feature that changes the footprint.

Mark the approximate centre of gravity when it is known. A tall cabinet, a motor, a safe and a long piece of machinery may be difficult for different reasons even when their total weights are similar. Record whether the weight is balanced, concentrated at one end or likely to shift during movement. Identify approved lifting points, strap locations and surfaces that must not be compressed or scratched.

The following cargo fields are useful stair climber quote requirements:

- Product name and intended final position.
- Gross weight of the complete transport unit.
- Packaged length, width and height.
- Centre-of-gravity position or an annotated drawing.
- Base shape, pallet footprint and contact points.
- Fragile, liquid-filled, loose or movable components.
- Required straps, protective pads or custom supports.
- Whether the same route will carry several cargo formats.

Never infer a safe capacity from a product title alone. Ask the supplier to confirm the applicable rated load, load position, attachment method and any residual capacity or route limitation in writing. The [LP800-1 400 kg product page](../products/lp800-1-electric-stair-climber-400kg.html) can be used as a reference point for a recorded model, but the published product information does not automatically approve every cargo or staircase.

## Document surfaces, weather and operating conditions

Describe every surface the stair climber will contact: concrete, tile, stone, metal, timber, carpet, wet pavement or unfinished construction flooring. Record dust, mud, oil, water, loose gravel, damaged nosings and temporary coverings. If conditions vary by season or shift, describe the worst normal condition and attach photographs. A route that is dry during the survey may not be dry during the planned operation.

Also record temperature exposure, rain protection, lighting, noise restrictions, available power and battery charging arrangements. Note whether the equipment must be stored on site, loaded into a vehicle, carried through a security gate or used in a customer-facing area. These details help the supplier discuss battery handling, charger compatibility, storage and cleaning requirements without inventing universal operating limits.

Describe the work pattern: occasional delivery, repeated daily transfers, a short project, warehouse replenishment or multi-site service. State how many loads are expected in a shift and whether the route must be cleared quickly between trips. Frequency is not a substitute for a manufacturer’s duty-cycle specification; it is information the supplier needs to select and confirm the configuration.

For general preparation, review the [safe heavy-item movement guide](../how-to-move-heavy-items-up-stairs.html). For a specific operation, the site survey should additionally identify who controls the route, who checks the load and how pedestrians are kept clear under the employer’s procedures.

## Separate confirmed measurements from supplier questions

A strong RFQ makes uncertainty visible. Create two columns: “measured or documented” and “supplier to confirm.” Put dimensions, photos, cargo records and route observations in the first column. Put model suitability, attachment design, turning method, braking behaviour, battery configuration, charging requirements, training documents, spare parts and after-sales support in the second column.

Ask the supplier to answer using the same field names and to identify assumptions. Request a written response to the following questions:

1. Is the proposed configuration suitable for the complete cargo weight and dimensions?
2. What load position, centre-of-gravity condition and attachment method apply to the rating?
3. Can the configuration be reviewed for the measured stair width, riser, tread and landing geometry?
4. Which route sections require photographs, drawings, a trial or an on-site assessment?
5. What is included: machine, battery, charger, straps, supports, controls and documentation?
6. What power and charging conditions apply at the destination?
7. Which operator training, inspection and maintenance documents are supplied?
8. Which spare parts, warranty terms and service response arrangements are available?
9. What packaging, crate, gross-shipping dimensions and handling marks will be provided?
10. Which claims require local regulations, destination-market review or a qualified safety professional?

A crawler, a wheel-based configuration and a turning-oriented design should not be treated as interchangeable without reviewing the route and cargo.

## Turn the survey into a comparable RFQ package

Send every supplier the same package: a one-page route sketch, the measurement table, cargo photographs, route photographs, operating frequency, destination market and the written-confirmation list. Use a revision date and a site reference so later quote changes remain traceable. If a measurement changes, issue a new revision rather than editing an old message without explanation.

Compare responses field by field. A useful comparison records the proposed model, configuration code, confirmed load conditions, open assumptions, included accessories, excluded work, documents supplied, warranty terms and quotation validity. This prevents a low headline price from hiding missing straps, charger options, delivery constraints or an unconfirmed route condition.

Distributors can use the [electric stair climber buyer guide](../blog/electric-stair-climber-buyer-guide.html) to explain basic equipment choices, while the site survey remains the customer-specific qualification record. If the project involves a known 400 kg model, link to the [stair climber operating guide](../stair-climber-guide.html) for background, but do not copy operating limits into a quotation unless the supplier confirms that they apply to the proposed configuration.

### A practical decision rule

Approve the RFQ for comparison when the route and cargo fields are complete, the supplier has answered the open questions and all assumptions are visible. Mark it “needs clarification” when a critical dimension, centre-of-gravity condition or access transition is missing. Mark it “do not recommend yet” when the supplier cannot explain the rating, attachment method or route limitation. This simple status system makes a site survey useful to procurement, sales and operations teams.

## FAQ: stair climber site survey and quote preparation

### What is the most important measurement before requesting a stair climber quote?

Start with the narrowest clear stair width, the complete cargo dimensions and the measured landing or turning space. These three records describe whether the machine and load can physically move through the route. Riser height, tread depth, door openings and thresholds should then be added for every section. Weight alone is not enough to approve a configuration, so ask the supplier to review the full route and load profile in writing.

### Should I measure the packaged cargo or the product itself?

Measure the packaged cargo as it will actually be transported. Include the pallet, crate, protective frame, removable handles and any part that changes the footprint or centre of gravity. If the product will be unpacked before movement, record both conditions and explain where unpacking occurs. The supplier should confirm which condition is used for the proposed load rating and attachment method.

### Do photographs replace a stair climber measurement checklist?

No. Photographs provide context but rarely show reliable scale, hidden thresholds or the narrowest clearance. Use photographs together with a sketch, written dimensions and labels that identify the route position. Include a reference object or a visible tape measure where helpful, but keep the measured values in the RFQ table so every supplier receives the same information.

### What should a supplier confirm in writing?

Ask the supplier to confirm the proposed model and configuration, applicable load conditions, attachment method, route limitations, battery and charger requirements, included accessories, training and inspection documents, warranty, spare parts and any required trial or site assessment. Ask the supplier to identify assumptions and missing information rather than treating silence as approval.

### How should a distributor collect customer information?

Use a standard intake form with route, cargo, access, work-pattern and destination-market fields. Request a route sketch, labelled photos and the complete packaged load profile before recommending a model. Store the form with a revision date and send the same information to each supplier. This creates a traceable record and reduces the risk of recommending equipment from an incomplete description.

### Can a product page confirm that a machine will work on my stairs?

A product page can provide documented model information, but it cannot replace a route review, operating instructions or supplier confirmation. Use the [stair climber category](../stair-climbers.html) and product pages to identify candidates, then submit your measurements, photos and cargo details. The final recommendation should state the confirmed conditions, open questions and any need for a trial or qualified site assessment.
