# How to Choose a Stair Climbing Machine by Load, Stairs and Format

**Direct answer:** Choose a stair climbing machine by matching the complete load to a documented capacity, then checking the stair surface, landings, route width, turning needs and the machine format. Start with the heaviest packaged load, not the product name. Next, compare silent wheels, training wheels, rubber rear wheels, inflatable wheels or crawler-type formats against the actual route. Finally, ask the supplier to confirm every missing site or configuration point before purchase. Chenma's referenced stair-climber records list documented maximum loads from 150 kg to 400 kg, but a capacity label alone does not prove suitability for every stair, surface or operating method.

This guide gives procurement teams a repeatable way to compare a **stair climber machine** without inventing a universal stair limit, price, certification claim or safety distance. Use it alongside the [Chenma stair-climber category](../stair-climbers.html) and the [commercial and industrial selection guide](../blog/electric-stair-climber-commercial-industrial-selection-guide.html).

## 1. Start with the complete load and route

A stair climbing machine moves a complete handling unit, not an abstract product weight. Record the item, packaging, straps, accessories and any support frame that will travel on the stairs. The maximum documented load is the first filter, but the route and load dimensions are equally important. A machine rated for 250 kg should not be treated as a 300 kg solution, and a 400 kg record should not be interpreted as approval for every 400 kg load shape.

Before comparing models, measure the load length, width and height. Note whether the load is rigid, flexible, top-heavy, fragile or difficult to secure. A route that accepts an empty machine may not accept the machine with a tall or wide package. Put these details into the RFQ so the supplier can review the complete handling condition.

## 2. Classify the stairs before selecting stair climbing equipment

A stair climber machine should be compared against the actual stairs rather than a generic “indoor” or “outdoor” label. Record the stair material, tread condition, landing size, changes in direction, doorway position, surface contamination and any exposed edge or transition. Also record whether the route includes a straight flight, intermediate landing, tight turn or a change from stairs to level floor.

The purpose of this inspection is not to create an unsupported universal stair limit. It is to identify the questions that the manufacturer must answer for the quoted configuration. Concrete, tile, stone, carpet, plastic, wet surfaces and uneven construction areas can present different handling conditions. Do not assume that a wheel or crawler format is suitable merely because its name sounds appropriate.

Use this stair and route checklist:

- Stair surface and condition, including wet, loose or contaminated areas.
- Tread and riser information where the supplier requests it.
- Stair width, landing dimensions and doorway openings.
- Direction changes, intermediate landings and final placement position.
- Load dimensions and the way the load will be secured.
- Operator position, visibility and any pedestrian interaction.
- Floor transitions at the top, bottom and between route sections.
- Site rules, local requirements and any professional review required before operation.

## 3. Compare wheel and crawler formats by task

The records describe several formats, including silent wheels, training wheels, rubber rear wheels, 30 cm inflatable large wheels and crawler-type wheels. These labels help organize a shortlist, but they do not replace route verification.

### Silent-wheel and training-wheel formats

LP800-1, LP800-101 and LP800-102 list silent wheels, manganese steel and stated dimensions of 54×62×180 cm. LP800-2 also lists the same dimensions and training wheels, with “easy to turn transport” as a stated feature. These records are useful starting points when the buyer wants to compare different documented load fields within a similar listed dimensional format. Confirm the full turning and load-handling method for the real stairs.

### Rubber-wheel and inflatable-wheel formats

DP800-1 lists rubber rear wheels and a stated all-terrain feature. DP800-2 lists rubber rear wheels and a stated U-turn-on-spot feature. DP2-800 lists 30 cm inflatable large wheels and an all-terrain cargo-hauling feature. These are source-record descriptions, not a promise that every outdoor surface, stair edge or construction route is suitable. Ask for route-specific confirmation and define how the load will be controlled.

### Crawler-type formats

LP-400 and LP-600 list crawler-type wheels. LP-400 lists a 60-degree tilt angle, while LP-600 lists 360° rotation. Treat these as documented product fields, not as a universal incline, turning or safety guarantee. A crawler stair climber still needs a review of the load, stair geometry, landings, transitions and operating procedure.

## 4. Use a step-by-step selection process

A repeatable decision flow reduces the risk of choosing by capacity alone:

1. **Write the complete load.** Include packaging, dimensions and the heaviest expected condition.
2. **Filter by documented maximum load.** The referenced records show 150 kg, 250 kg, 300 kg and 400 kg entries.
3. **Map the route.** Record stair material, width, landings, doors, turns, transitions and placement point.
4. **Shortlist the format.** Compare silent wheels, training wheels, rubber rear wheels, inflatable wheels and crawler-type records against the route questions.
5. **Check the operating method.** Explain loading, securing, ascent, descent, turning, parking and unloading in the RFQ.
6. **Request configuration confirmation.** Ask the supplier to confirm every parameter that is absent, conditional or unclear in the product record.
7. **Keep the written review.** Store the site measurements, selected model, load description and unresolved questions with the quotation.

This process answers “how to choose a stair climbing machine” without pretending that one model or format works for every building.

## 5. Build a practical transfer safety checklist

Stair climbing equipment should be treated as part of a controlled transfer operation. Before a move, verify that the load is secured, the route is clear, the operator can see or communicate through the route and the landing is ready to receive the load. Confirm who controls the area when pedestrians, doors or other equipment share the route.

The checklist should also cover the machine condition, battery or power arrangement stated for the selected record, wheels or crawlers, controls, straps and any site-specific inspection requirement. Do not infer a brake, overload protection, safety factor, certification scope or operating limit unless it is stated in the applicable record or confirmed by the manufacturer and responsible local professional.

If a route includes an unknown condition, stop the selection process at that point and put the question into the RFQ. “Confirm by local regulations and manufacturer data” is a valid procurement action; a guessed threshold is not.

## 6. Prepare an RFQ that produces a useful answer

A category-only enquiry makes it difficult to compare stair climber machines. Send a structured brief containing:

- Maximum complete load and normal load.
- Package length, width, height and center-of-load information if available.
- Stair surface, route width, landings, turns, doors and floor transitions.
- Required format preference, such as silent wheels, rubber wheels, inflatable wheels or crawler-type.
- Operating frequency and the planned loading, ascent, descent and unloading method.
- Available power and any battery or charger requirement that must be confirmed.
- Destination country, site procedure and documentation requirements.
- Photos or a measured route sketch, where permitted by the site.
- Every unknown parameter that requires written manufacturer confirmation.
