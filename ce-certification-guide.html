For buyers and importers of material handling equipment, compliance is not optional. Understanding which certifications your supplier must hold is the first step to a smooth customs clearance and a safe operation site in the EU.

Direct Answer: The Compliance Rule

Before importing material handling equipment into the European Union, verify that your supplier provides CE-marked products assessed against the Machinery Directive, with safety requirements such as EN ISO 3691-1 for industrial trucks.

What Does CE Marking Mean for Buyers?

The CE mark indicates that a product meets applicable EU safety, health, and environmental requirements. For material handling equipment such as stair climbers, electric forklifts, stackers, and lifting platforms, this typically involves the Machinery Directive and its harmonized standards.

  • Legal Market Access: CE-marked equipment can be legally sold and operated within the European Economic Area.
  • Safety Assurance: The assessment process verifies mechanical, electrical, and functional safety of the equipment.
  • Risk Reduction: CE compliance reduces liability for both the importer and the end user in case of incidents.

Key Questions to Ask Your Supplier

  • Is every model CE-certified? Certification should cover the full product line, not just a single model.
  • Which standards were applied? For industrial trucks, harmonized standards such as EN ISO 3691-1 define the safety requirements.
  • Are OEM/ODM projects covered? Confirm that custom-branded equipment undergoes the same compliance process.
  • What documentation is provided? A Declaration of Conformity and technical documentation should accompany each shipment.
